Tekly Tasks: from Orderry copy to “next steps that follow the job”

Each tab is one phase. The screen shows what staff will actually see in Tekly, drawn in Tekly’s own colours, font and layout. Numbered dots match the notes beside it. All names and numbers are examples.

What the council and market research decided

Four reviewers (shop owner, counter and bench staff, architect, product strategist) went through every feature. Then we checked what competitors actually do and what makes shops money.

  • About 40% of the first plan already exists in Tekly. That includes automations, customer text rules, per-status time limits, Sense’s morning brief and quote follow-ups, the orders board, Go Today, ⌘K, checklists, and the tracking page with quote approval and deposits. We build on these and don’t rebuild them.
  • Nobody buys repair software for its to-do list. The value has to show up as approved quotes and collected, paid devices.
  • No competitor documents these five: a pickup chain that stops by itself, one sequence mixing customer texts and staff calls, a “money recovered” report, turnaround clocks that pause on their own, and money waiting by age.
  • Three hidden bugs must be fixed first. Task alerts never reach the bell. Timed automations never run on their own. A delayed text can reach a customer who has already collected.
One line vs Orderry: Tekly chases every stalled repair for you and stops the moment the job moves

Build order

  1. 0Foundations: task bugs, alerts to the bell and push, timed automations, a check before delayed texts send.
  2. 1Follow-ups: ready but not collected, and quote not answered. A Next steps card on the job, a preview, and a “money recovered” report.
  3. 2Waiting board: money waiting by age, clocks that pause, a wall-screen mode.
  4. 3My day: one list per person on web that matches Go Today.
  5. 4Later, after talking to beta shops: shift routines, supplier returns, a counter filter.

Test before Phase 1 screens: check beta shops’ data for money stuck in jobs ready over 3 days and quotes waiting over 2 days. Ask 3 owners to approve a daily reminder list for 2 weeks. Make five short calls about how the counter is staffed.

Phase 0: foundations

The same page, with nothing broken or hidden. Staff open Tasks and see their own work first.

Behind the scenes it fixes three things the follow-ups need:

  • Task alerts reach the bell and phone push. Today they only go by email or text.
  • Timed automations run every hour on their own.
  • A delayed customer text is checked again before it sends, so nobody gets a pickup reminder after collecting.
About a weekAlso removes an old task API that has no permission checks

What changes

  1. 1“Mine” is the default. Search finds a task by text, job number or customer.
  2. 2Automatic tasks get a real status, and a small “Auto” tag so you know where they came from.
  3. 3Links show the job and the customer together.
  4. 4The panel edits everything: title, due date, priority, notes. Delete goes to a 30-day bin.
  5. 5Every assignee gets the notification and their share of commission.

Phase 1: follow-ups that chase waiting jobs

When a job goes into a waiting state, Tekly texts the customer on a schedule and puts a call on the right person’s list. When the job moves on, the follow-up stops by itself. Nobody types these tasks.

It’s built from Tekly’s existing automation and text rules, so there’s no second texting engine. The tracking page already lets customers approve quotes and pay deposits, so every text carries that link.

New: a “money recovered” report shows what the follow-ups brought back. No competitor shows this.

1–2 weeksOff by default · preview firstService texts only, quiet hours respected

What you’ll see

  1. 1Inside the work order: a “Next step” card shows what Tekly will do and when.
  2. 2A timeline shows what was already sent, so nobody texts the customer twice.
  3. 3Settings → Follow-ups: turn each recipe on or off and change the days.
  4. 4Before switching on, Tekly shows exactly which jobs would be affected today.
  5. 5A report of what the follow-ups recovered, which proves Tekly pays for itself. The morning summary goes into Sense’s existing brief instead of a new email.

Phase 3: “My day”, one list per person

The web gets what Tekly Go’s Today screen already does. Each person opens Tasks and sees today. Counter staff see the follow-up calls, and techs see their bench jobs with “part arrived” pinned on top. These are filters on one screen, not separate queues.

Follow-up steps wake up by themselves when the customer replies or a part arrives, because Phase 1 already ties them to the job. No separate snooze system is needed.

2 weeksSame view on web and Tekly GoOwner gets “Everyone’s day”

What you’ll see

  1. 1Today / Upcoming / Later instead of one long table.
  2. 2Late items are a calm “Catch up” group with a one-click “Move all to today”, not a red wall.
  3. 3A follow-up came back early because the customer replied.
  4. 4A waiting job hides until its part arrives. That comes from the job, with nothing to set.
  5. 5One due date and a simple repeat. No second date field.

Phase 2: the waiting board

The owner’s one daily screen. It’s the existing work-order board, grouped by what each job is waiting for, with the money stuck in each group and how long it has been stuck. Turnaround clocks pause by themselves while a job waits on parts or the customer, using Tekly’s existing per-status time limits.

No competitor documents money waiting by age or clocks that pause by themselves.

2–3 weeksBuilt on Work orders → BoardWall mode shows no money

What you’ll see

  1. 1Columns by what the job waits for: customer, parts, bench.
  2. 2Money waiting and age on every card, oldest first.
  3. 3A handoff is marked “New for you”. Opening or starting the job counts as accepting, with no extra button.
  4. 4The turnaround clock pauses while waiting on parts.
  5. 5Wall mode: the same board in big type for a back-room screen, without amounts.

These are real needs, but we found no proof yet that shops will move them off the whiteboard. We ask beta shops first (a photo of their whiteboard, and how many supplier returns they do each month), then build on Tekly’s existing checklist model.

Cut: what we won’t build, and why

This was the original Phase 5 screen. The council cut most of it because it adds noise, or because Tekly already has it.

  • AI assignee suggestion. In a small shop the answer is always the job’s tech. A fixed rule does that.
  • Voice drafts. About 62% accuracy in the best-known product, so checking a draft takes longer than typing it.
  • A new ⌘K. Tekly already has one. If it’s nearly free, “Add task” becomes one more command, with no sentence parser.
  • Renaming Tasks to “Next steps”. It confuses shops switching from Orderry. Only the card on the job uses that name.
  • Handoff Accept button, two-person ticks, a second date field, rotating counter owner, per-task checklists, a separate morning email for techs. Each adds clicks or settings someone has to keep up.

  4. 4Voice draft. Cut for now.
